Three ideas describe a stream's metabolism

Like a body, a river takes in energy and spends it. Oxygen in the water lets us measure both sides, every day, without disturbing the stream.

Making

Algae and water plants use sunlight to build food and release oxygen. Scientists call this gross primary production, or GPP. It rises on sunny days and drops when floods scour the riverbed.

Using

Microbes, insects, fish and plants all breathe, using up oxygen. This is ecosystem respiration, or ER. Warm water and plenty of fallen leaves speed it up.

Balance

Making minus using is the net balance (NEP). When a stream makes more than it uses, it grows its own food. When it uses more, it depends on leaves and organic matter washing in.

What this can say about river health

Metabolism is one clue about how a river is working. It is most useful when read together with water quality, flow and what lives in the stream.

Makes more than it uses

A lot of algae are growing, usually in sunny, open, nutrient-rich water. Some of this is natural. Very high production can swing oxygen sharply between day and night, which stresses fish and insects.

Close to balanced

Food made in the stream roughly matches what the stream consumes. Many healthy rivers sit here in summer and shift with the seasons, with production peaking in the long days after spring floods.

Uses more than it makes

The stream runs on food from outside, such as leaves, soil and upstream inputs. That is normal in shaded mountain reaches. In town it can also point to extra organic material, so we compare it with nearby sites.

Read with care. These are estimates from a computer model of oxygen swings, not direct counts. They are least certain in fast, steep, bubbly water, where oxygen exchanges with the air so quickly that the signal is faint. A site's number alone is not a grade for the river.

Words used here

g O₂ per m² per day
Grams of oxygen made or used per square metre of riverbed in one day. It lets different sites be compared fairly.
GPP
Gross primary production: oxygen made by algae and plants.
ER
Ecosystem respiration: oxygen used by everything living in the stream. Shown as a positive amount in the public view.
NEP
Net ecosystem production: oxygen made minus oxygen used.
Production-to-respiration ratio
Oxygen made divided by oxygen used. Above 1 means the stream makes more than it uses.
Sonde
A sensor package left in the river to measure oxygen, temperature, depth and more.
Estimates are modeled from continuous sonde data. Sondes are pulled in winter, so there are no estimates from about November to March.
